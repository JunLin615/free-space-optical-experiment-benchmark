# Benchmark landscape for free-space optical experimental reasoning

Research snapshot: 28 September 2026. This is a scoped survey of primary papers, project repositories, datasets, and official evaluation documentation, not a claim that every unpublished or private benchmark has been found. “Reported” below means a claim made by the cited project; “Recommendation” is a design inference for this project. Benchmark names and task counts can change, so a future publication should recheck the linked sources and record the survey date.

## Finding and search boundary

**No close, public equivalent was found in this search.** The nearest projects address geometric-optics images, optical knowledge questions, lens optimization, nanophotonic design, or control of optical devices. The combination sought here is different: free-space *experimental* reasoning across setup design, beam-path and polarization reasoning, diagnosis, calibration, quantitative prediction, and verification; comparable protocols for agents with or without retrieval and tools; and autonomous scoring independent of one optical software package. This is a bounded negative finding, not proof of nonexistence.

Searches covered project repositories, arXiv, publisher pages, and Hugging Face datasets for combinations of *optics*, *photonics*, *geometric optics*, *free-space optics*, *optical bench*, *optical design*, *agent*, *benchmark*, and *simulation*. “FSO benchmark” results frequently concern communications links rather than tabletop free-space experiments. These ambiguous terms make title-only screening unreliable.

## Closest optics and photonics work

Each row separates a source-supported observation from what this project might adopt. “Avoid” is a scope or evaluation choice for *our* benchmark; it is not a criticism of the cited work in its intended domain.

| Project and primary source | Reported scope and evaluation | Recommendation: useful pattern | Avoid copying |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [GOBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00991), [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bonnot/GOBench) | Geometric-optics image generation and visual understanding, with scenarios involving direct light, reflection, and refraction. The paper reports subjective ratings for generated imagery and accuracy for understanding tasks. | Include diagram interpretation as a distinct capability if the seed warrants it; check optical authenticity separately from visual plausibility. | Do not turn a bench-design and diagnosis benchmark into image aesthetics or elementary ray-diagram recognition. Subjective image ratings do not satisfy autonomous core scoring. |
| [OpticsBench aberration benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15499) | Zernike-derived optical-aberration corruptions test image-classifier robustness to realistic blur. A [subsequent paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18510) extends the study to detection and lens corruptions. | Model aberrations realistically where image quality is an actual case outcome. This name is already occupied. | Do not treat image-classifier robustness as an optics agent reasoning score or adopt the name “OpticsBench.” |
| [OpticsBench ray-optics simulator](https://opticsbench.com/) | A separately named browser platform advertises non-sequential ray tracing, lenses, mirrors, prisms, filters, detectors, and other optical modelling features. It is a *tool*, not an agent benchmark in its site description. | Inspect whether an external simulator can serve as an optional implementation or cross-check for some cases. | Do not make the benchmark dependent on this site's features, fidelity claims, or availability; avoid its name. |
| [OptiBench virtual lab](https://github.com/thepacket/optibench) | An experimental browser-based virtual photonics lab with simulated benches and instruments. Its README expressly notes no independent laboratory validation. | A useful example of instrument-like observations and model-limit disclosures for future interactive tracks. | Do not use an unvalidated simulator as the sole gold oracle, or adopt the near-colliding name “OptiBench.” |
| [OPTICS](https://github.com/AbhishekKolari/OPTICS) | Despite its name, this is a visual object-property reasoning benchmark, including counting and comparison tasks; it is not about optical physics. | Use it as a concrete check against title-only literature screening and as another naming-collision warning. | Do not borrow its object-property task taxonomy or use “OPTICS” as this benchmark's name. |
| [OptoChat / OptoQA / OptoMCQA](https://doi.org/10.1088/2515-7647/ae7491) | An optics retrieval system evaluated with 580 generated-answer QA pairs and 252 multiple-choice questions. The paper reports ROUGE-L and UniEval for QA, accuracy for MCQA, and expert assessment of generated answers. | Compare closed-book and retrieval-assisted protocols on the *same* questions; retain source provenance. | Do not equate text similarity or factual recall with experimental design correctness. Do not require per-run expert assessment. |
| [OptiDesignQA / OPTIAGENT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23761) | Dataset and agent work for optical *lens-system* synthesis and completion. The paper describes format, physical-feasibility, light-manipulation, and heuristic rewards plus specialized optimization. | Use machine-readable design specifications and physical-feasibility checks where applicable. | Do not restrict the benchmark to refractive imaging lenses, one optimizer, or a reward function trained for one agent. |
| [LensArena / LensDesigner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30450) | arXiv records v1 as submitted **24 September 2026**, before this snapshot. The paper reports 120 lens-design tasks and an interactive simulation/retrieval loop. Its abstract says code **will** be public; public code availability was not independently established in this survey. | Consider objective, simulation-measured performance and efficiency for design cases. Track this benchmark as it matures. | Do not treat an announced suite as a verified released dataset, or equate lens optimization with wider tabletop experimental reasoning. |
| [HALO-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28877) | The paper reports 52 nanophotonic-design tasks using typed specifications, electromagnetic simulation, diagnostics, and comparisons among structured and coding agents; it also examines reuse of failed trajectories and token use. | Typed outputs, simulator feedback, and explicit operational-failure categories are valuable patterns. | Purely on-chip/nanophotonic design lies outside the proposed core scope; do not require its electromagnetic simulator or planner interface. |
| [AgentOptics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20144) | The paper reports a 410-task benchmark for language-directed optical-device control with 64 MCP tools across eight devices, emphasizing coordination, linguistic robustness, and error handling. Its showcased systems center on communications and fiber devices. | Separate *action execution* from *physical goal satisfaction* when evaluating tool-using agents. | Do not make success depend on MCP, device-specific command syntax, or fiber-communications tasks. |
| [OptoMate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17985) | A research platform for robot assembly and alignment of tabletop free-space optical experiments. This is a close **application**, not a general-purpose public reasoning benchmark in the cited abstract. | Physical assembly and alignment provide realistic failure modes and observable endpoints for future embodied tracks. | Do not require access to a robot or copy its hardware assumptions into the core question set. |

Other optical work may be worth later inspection, but a title containing “optical” does not establish benchmark fit. For instance, the cited [GOBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00991) concerns geometric-optics perception while [HALO-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28877) concerns nanophotonic devices. A public novelty claim should say *“we did not find a close equivalent under the documented search”*, with a dated search log, rather than *“the first optics benchmark.”*

## Physics, science, and agent benchmark patterns

| Project and primary source | Reported fact relevant here | Recommendation: borrow | Avoid copying |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [PHYSICS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21821) and [PHYSICS-Verified](https://github.com/yale-nlp/physics-verified) | PHYSICS covers university-level physics including optics. The later audited edition has 1,034 qualifying-exam problems and 2,559 individually scored answers. Its authors removed prompts that require proofs, explanations, drawings, or unavailable figures and use labeled final answers plus an LLM judge. The repository documents judge perturbation self-tests, cross-judge checks, partial credit, and judge-dependent scores. | Score subquestions independently; explicitly represent answer type, units, tolerances, and ambiguity. Test the judge with correct variants, near misses, omissions, and swapped labels; record judge version. | Do not inherit a results-only policy wholesale: this project needs setup, diagnosis, and design. Do not assume an LLM judge is an objective physical oracle, or discard every open-ended case before attempting a verifiable output contract. |
| [ScienceAgentBench](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/ScienceAgentBench) | 102 data-driven scientific tasks from 44 papers are unified as self-contained Python programs; evaluation examines programs, execution, and costs. The repository reports a verified release to reduce false negatives and a containerized harness. | Require concrete, independently checkable artifacts when a task can naturally produce them; version corrected reference artifacts and report cost. | Do not force every optics case into Python code or make paper reproduction the task. Its documented human curation is a construction method, not a necessary per-run scoring gate here. |
| [Terminal-Bench-Science](https://github.com/harbor-framework/terminal-bench-science) | Scientific terminal workflows are run in sandboxed environments. Its README asks users to run oracle solutions repeatedly to detect flaky tasks and describes task-version releases; its [leaderboard](https://www.terminal-bench-science.ai/) reports resolution, tokens, and cost. | Test validators and oracle submissions repeatedly; isolate execution; version environments and record efficiency alongside success. | Do not make a terminal, particular container provider, or scientific software install the scientific definition of an optics question. Do not mistake a task-specific test passing for comprehensive physical validity. |
| [OlympiadBench](https://github.com/OpenBMB/OlympiadBench) | Bilingual multimodal mathematics and physics competition problems. The project documents answer types, units, multiple-answer metadata, standardized open-answer scoring, and sample assessment for proof questions. | Distinguish numeric, symbolic, set-valued, and multi-part answers; preserve diagrams as data where needed. | Do not frame laboratory troubleshooting as an Olympiad problem, or leave proof-like cases on a manual-scoring track in the core benchmark. |
| [AgentIdeaBench](https://github.com/HKUST-KnowComp/AgentIdeaBench) | Scientific ideation is compared under matched static-reference and agent-controlled search protocols. The same output format and critics are used in both. Its scoring includes multidimensional anchored critic ratings and retrieved prior-art evidence. | Keep optical questions identical when varying case-library or retrieval access; record retrieved material and tool budget. Use grounded, narrow semantic criteria where physics checks cannot resolve a claim. | Do not substitute novelty, impact, or generic idea quality for optical feasibility and requirement satisfaction. A search-based critic cannot establish a beam path's physical correctness. |
| [PhysGym](https://github.com/principia-ai/PhysGym) | 97 executable physics environments allow budgeted interactive experiments at four prior-knowledge levels. The repository lists equation-equivalence and predictive-fit metrics, including symbolic plus model-based equivalence, R², MSE, Kendall's tau, and MAPE. | Parameterize physically meaningful hidden instances; score predictions on withheld inputs and count experiment calls. Vary information exposure without altering the physical task. | Do not demand equation discovery from every agent or anonymize variables in ways that erase normal experimental context. Simulator fidelity and range limits must be explicit. |
| [Inspect AI](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/scoring.html) | An evaluation framework with datasets, solvers, scorers, tools, sandboxes, logs, model grading, multiple scores, and metrics. Its documentation distinguishes task failures from unscored infrastructure failures. | Define a thin adapter to a general harness and retain raw traces and score provenance; keep scoring policy explicit. | Do not put the scientific case schema inside one framework's Python API or claim that a framework's scorer provides scientific validity by itself. |
| [PaperBench](https://github.com/openai/frontier-evals/blob/main/project/paperbench/README.md) | Research replication tasks use hierarchical rubric leaves, separate rollout/reproduction/grading stages, and an automated model judge; the project also evaluates judge quality. | Decompose open-ended design claims into observable criteria and keep agent generation separate from independent scoring. | Do not use a single broad model-judge grade as the headline physical correctness score, or assume a rubric can rescue an underspecified prompt. |

## What the evaluation literature implies for this project

The following are **recommendations**, not features already present in the 64-case seed.

1. **Match verification to claim type.** Numeric predictions should use independent equations or reference solvers, units, and declared tolerances. Symbolic claims should use algebraic checks plus sampled counterexamples. Ordered paths and optical states can be checked against typed graph constraints. Observable performance can use an independent simulator within a declared validity region. A semantic judge should handle only residual criteria that cannot yet be operationalized, and its score should remain separately visible.
2. **Do not conflate a parsable answer with a correct one.** A structured output contract helps extract component relationships, node states, predicted observables, and calibration steps. The evaluator still needs independent physics and constraint checks. Free text can remain for explanation, while the scoring contract permits multiple valid topologies and aliases.
3. **Use a case-specific evidence ladder.** A result can be verified directly, checked over multiple parameter samples, evaluated by a validated simulation, checked against a structured constraint graph, or semantically judged with calibrated model judges. Label which evidence supports each criterion. A judge-only criterion is weaker evidence than an executable physical check.
4. **Calibrate model judges as instruments.** Follow the pattern of [PHYSICS-Verified](https://github.com/yale-nlp/physics-verified) and [PaperBench](https://openai.com/index/paperbench/): build a frozen set of correct variants and adversarial near misses; measure false acceptance and false rejection; compare independent judges; pin prompt/model/version; publish criterion-level uncertainty and disagreement. A majority vote can reduce some noise but cannot correct a shared physical misconception. No human must grade normal runs.
5. **Check the benchmark's tests, not only agents.** An oracle solution should pass, an intentionally invalid or no-op solution should fail, and perturbations should exercise boundary tolerances and alternative valid architectures. Repeat stochastic tools, as [Terminal-Bench-Science](https://github.com/harbor-framework/terminal-bench-science) recommends for its oracle runs. False negatives in [ScienceAgentBench's verified update](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/ScienceAgentBench) and the curation of [PHYSICS-Verified](https://github.com/yale-nlp/physics-verified) show that reference errors are a substantive benchmark risk.
6. **Separate capability from resource use.** Report success and criterion scores alongside input/output/cached tokens, tool calls, wall time, retries, and priced cost where available. Compare closed-book, case-assisted, tool-assisted, and combined protocols on the same case IDs and generation seeds. [AgentIdeaBench](https://github.com/HKUST-KnowComp/AgentIdeaBench) demonstrates matched protocol comparisons; [ScienceAgentBench](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/ScienceAgentBench) and [Terminal-Bench-Science](https://www.terminal-bench-science.ai/) report execution/resource measures. Avoid one opaque composite score.
7. **Keep public science and protected tests distinct.** Publish the task definition, schema, sample validators, and development cases. For leaderboard use, hold out concrete parameter seeds, variants, and where appropriate complete instances and reference outputs. A hidden test is legitimate only when its required behavior follows from the public problem statement. Document generator and evaluator versions and audit leaks. Publicly readable tests and repeated exposure can narrow what a benchmark measures.

## Gap to fill and conditions on the claim

The opportunity is an optics-native benchmark whose **unit of assessment is a physically meaningful experimental objective**, not a single prose reference answer, a preferred software workflow, or one topology. Cases should state the observables, constraints, tolerances, and allowed design freedom needed for an autonomous evaluator. A benchmark of this sort would complement the cited optics work: it would test cross-domain free-space experimental reasoning and design under matched access protocols while measuring both physical validity and resource use.

That opportunity is conditional. Some current open-ended prompts may not admit defensible autonomous scoring as written. They should remain provisional until the problem statement and scoreable outputs define what counts as success, a plausible alternative design passes, and an adversarial invalid design fails. The benchmark should report how many cases reach each verification level rather than implying that an LLM judge resolves every ambiguity. This is a design problem for the next round, not a reason to require a human grader during normal execution.
